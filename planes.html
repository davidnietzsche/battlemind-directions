19 · Paper Planes — the take-off experience

On load

The four headline words rise in one after another, the man fades up, and the first plane leaves his hand. From then on planes launch continuously along the arc, each banking into the curve. The button lands last.

Mouse

Three parallax depths: the dot grid barely moves, the headline a little, the man more. The flight arc itself bends toward the cursor, so visitors steer the stream of planes without being told to.

Scroll

The hero is pinned for one extra screen. Scrolling accelerates the planes and lifts the arc out of frame while the headline drifts up slower and the man sinks away. The dotted flight line draws itself into the next section, where the planes land on the three proof numbers and pop them in one by one.

WebGL layer

The planes are real 3D paper darts (three.js): two folded wings and a keel, matte brand red, lit from the top left, with banking, scale change as they fly toward the camera, and a soft ambient term so red never goes orange. Everything else stays DOM, so text is selectable and indexable.

Fallbacks

  • No WebGL: SVG planes on the same path (GSAP MotionPath), same timing.
  • Reduced motion: the composition sits still with the planes placed along the arc; the proof cards simply appear.
  • Phones: the arc is steeper and shorter, the man moves to the right edge, no pin.

Cost

One small WebGL scene (12 meshes, one light), GSAP + ScrollTrigger. Hero stays under 1 MB with the cut-out as webp. 60 fps target on a 2019 laptop and a mid-range phone; the scene pauses when off-screen.
