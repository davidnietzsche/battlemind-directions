Three mascots, one per service

From the 24 Nov meeting: the mascots carry the site, three of them, one per service (performance marketing now includes digital advertising). Each has to say what it does at a glance, and the bull has to be as simple as the others. Below: the reason for each, the options, the set I recommend, twenty-four more mascot types, and five more styles to choose from. See the recommended set on the page.

Performance marketing

A bull, because performance marketing should run like a bull market: the numbers only go up. It now covers digital advertising too, so it carries the tools of both: reach (the megaphone) and results (the rising bars, or the target and the arrow).

Simplified as Sue asked: a plain rounded head, two dot eyes, a small muzzle. No wrinkles, no fur, no muscles, so it sits in one set with the others.

Creative design & video

The clapperboard is its head, Sue's idea: every shoot starts with the clapperboard, so nobody has to ask why this one is creative. It carries the camera it shoots with.

Option B makes the head a camera with one big lens for an eye, which is just as clear but reads more as "video" than "design".

Web & app development

A robot, because it builds and runs your website and apps, and never sleeps. It holds the laptop and the phone it builds for.

Unchanged: Sue and Ian both said it already reads straight away.

Five more styles

The same three characters, each made five other ways. Pick a style first; the characters can then be refined in it.

3D clay

Handmade plasticine, like a stop-motion puppet: fingerprints, soft matte, warm. The friendliest of the five.

Plush toy

Sewn felt and velvet with stitched seams and embroidered eyes. Soft and huggable; reads well as merchandise too.

Flat sticker

Bold 2D vector with thick outlines and flat colour. The lightest to load, and easy to reuse on social posts, slides and stickers.

Inflatable

Glossy balloon vinyl, like a giant pool toy. Playful and bright, close to the current 3D look but softer.

Origami paper

Folded paper with sharp low-poly facets. Crafted and a little editorial; it stands out most from other agencies' 3D mascots.
